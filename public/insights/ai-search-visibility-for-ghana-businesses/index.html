AI search visibility for growing businesses: a plain-English guide

What AI search actually is, how it differs from traditional SEO, what to realistically expect and how to judge anyone offering to help.

By Joshua Abbey, Founder · 1 October 2026 · 5 min read

"AI search visibility" is one of those phrases that gets used a lot and explained very little. If you run a business in Ghana, you may have heard that ChatGPT and Google's AI answers are "changing search" without anyone telling you what that means for your enquiries next month. This guide is a plain-English explanation: what has changed, how it differs from traditional SEO, what you can realistically expect, and how to judge anyone who offers to help.

What has actually changed

Search used to be a list of ten blue links. You typed a query, scanned the results and clicked. Today, there are several other ways people get answers:

  • AI summaries in Google. For many questions, Google shows an AI-generated overview above the regular results, often answering the question directly.
  • AI assistants. Tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity answer questions conversationally and can search the web as they go, citing the sources they used.
  • Voice and in-app assistants on phones, which tend to give one answer rather than a list.

In each case, the user receives a short, synthesised answer. They may still click through to a website, or they may act directly on what the assistant said: calling the business it named, or asking a follow-up question about it. "AI search visibility" simply means how often, and how accurately, your business shows up in those answers.

How it differs from traditional SEO

Traditional SEO focuses on ranking a particular page for a particular keyword. AI visibility overlaps heavily with that, because the assistants often read the same top-ranking pages. But there are differences in emphasis:

  • Entities, not just pages. AI tools try to understand your business as a thing: its name, location, services, reputation and how it relates to others. Consistent facts across many sources matter more.
  • Answers, not just keywords. Content that directly answers a question in clear language is easier for an assistant to use and cite.
  • Reputation everywhere. Reviews, mentions and listings on other websites help confirm that you are a credible recommendation.
  • Fewer slots. A results page shows many options; an AI answer may name only a handful. Being "pretty good" is less useful than being clearly the best fit for a specific question.

The upshot: good SEO is still the foundation, and businesses with solid websites, profiles and reviews tend to do better in AI answers too. But it pays to deliberately shape your information for question-and-answer use.

Why it matters in Ghana specifically

Many sectors in Ghana are still lightly documented online. Plenty of good clinics, schools, caterers and service firms have little more than a social media page and an unclaimed map listing. That creates a real opportunity. When an assistant looks for a "reliable private school in Tema with a strong STEM programme" and finds only one school with a clear website, a detailed admissions FAQ and plenty of recent parent reviews, that school is the obvious answer.

The diaspora adds another layer. Ghanaians abroad researching property, schools for relatives or event vendors for a wedding back home are heavy users of online research, and increasingly of AI tools. They can't drive past your office, so the information they can find online is everything.

What good AI visibility work involves

Whether you do it yourself or hire help, the work usually falls into five areas:

  1. Baseline. Agree a list of real customer questions and record how the main assistants answer them today, including which competitors they name.
  2. Website clarity. Make sure each service has its own clear page, with location, pricing guidance, process and FAQs written in plain language.
  3. Structured data. Add schema markup so machines can read your business details, services and FAQs reliably.
  4. Profiles and listings. Complete and align your Google Business Profile, social profiles and relevant directories, with identical name, address and phone details.
  5. Reputation. Build a steady flow of genuine reviews and look for credible mentions on other sites, from industry bodies to local press and partners.

Then repeat the baseline check every month and adjust.

What to realistically expect

Be clear-eyed about three things. First, nobody can guarantee that an AI assistant will recommend you; the systems are owned and changed by large technology companies. Second, answers vary: ask the same question twice and you may get different names. Look at trends over months rather than single answers. Third, this is cumulative work. Website and profile fixes can be done in weeks; reputation grows over months.

What you should see over time is more frequent and more accurate mentions for the questions you care about, and more enquiries from people who say "I found you through ChatGPT" or "Google's summary mentioned you". It's worth adding "How did you hear about us?" to your enquiry form to capture this.

How to judge an agency's offer

As interest grows, so do inflated promises. Some practical questions to ask anyone offering AI visibility services:

  • Which questions will you track, and in which assistants? You want a specific, agreed list, not vague claims.
  • What will you change on my website and profiles? Real work should be concrete and visible.
  • How will you report? Expect a monthly record of answers and mentions, not just a single "score".
  • Do you guarantee recommendations? The honest answer is no. Be cautious of anyone who says yes.
  • Do you create fake reviews or mentions? This should be an immediate no. It can damage your reputation and break platform rules.

Getting started

You can make meaningful progress in a single afternoon: ask three assistants about your business and your service area, fix any wrong or inconsistent details you find, and write down the ten questions customers ask you most. Those answers are the beginning of your AI-ready content.
