Local SEO in Ghana: getting the most from your Google Business Profile

How to claim, complete and maintain your Google Business Profile so nearby customers find you in the map pack, trust you and get in touch.

By Joshua Abbey, Founder · 1 October 2026 · 5 min read

When someone in Accra searches "pharmacy near me", "pediatric dentist Spintex" or "best jollof in Osu", Google usually shows a map with three businesses underneath it before any ordinary website links. That map section is often called the local pack, and for businesses that serve customers in a specific area, it is some of the most valuable space on the internet.

The businesses in that pack are pulled largely from Google Business Profile, the free listing that powers your presence on Google Search and Google Maps. This guide explains how to set up, optimise and maintain your profile so nearby customers find you, trust you and get in touch.

How Google decides who appears locally

Google has said publicly that local results depend mainly on three things:

  • Relevance: how well your profile matches what the person searched for.
  • Distance: how far your business is from the searcher or the location in their search.
  • Prominence: how well known and well regarded your business is, based on reviews, links, mentions and other signals.

You can't move your premises closer to every customer, but you have a lot of control over relevance and prominence. That is what local SEO is about.

Step 1: Claim and verify your profile

Search for your business name on Google Maps. If a listing already exists, perhaps added by a customer, claim it. If not, create one at Google Business Profile. Google will ask you to verify that you really operate the business. Methods vary, but recorded video verification is common: a short, continuous video that shows your signage, your location in context and proof that you manage the business, such as your work area or equipment.

A few tips for a smooth verification: make sure your signage is visible and matches your business name, have business registration documents to hand, and use the exact name you trade under, without adding keywords.

If you serve customers at their location rather than at a shop, such as a caterer, cleaner or mobile mechanic, set yourself up as a service-area business and hide your address while listing the areas you cover.

Step 2: Complete every relevant field

An incomplete profile is one of the most common reasons businesses miss out. Work through:

  • Business name: your real-world name, exactly as on your signage. Adding keywords ("ABC Clinic Best Dentist Accra") breaks Google's guidelines and can get your profile suspended.
  • Primary category: the single most important choice. Pick the most specific category that describes your main business ("Pediatric dentist" rather than just "Dentist" if that's your focus). Add relevant secondary categories too.
  • Address and map pin: accurate, with the pin exactly on your entrance. Mention your Ghana Post GPS digital address and a clear landmark in your description, because many customers still navigate by landmarks.
  • Phone and website: a number that is answered during business hours, and a link to the most relevant page on your site.
  • Hours: including special hours for public holidays such as Independence Day and Christmas.
  • Services or products: list them individually with short descriptions and prices or price ranges where possible.
  • Description: a clear summary of what you do, who you serve and where, written for people rather than search engines.
  • Attributes: options such as wheelchair access, online appointments or delivery, where relevant.

Step 3: Show your business with real photos

Customers want to see what they are getting. Upload clear, recent photos of your exterior (so people recognise the building), interior, team, products and work. Add new photos regularly; a profile with fresh, genuine images looks active and trustworthy. Avoid stock photos and heavy text overlays.

Step 4: Build a steady flow of genuine reviews

Reviews influence both your visibility and whether people choose you. The key is consistency: a steady trickle of recent reviews beats a burst from years ago.

  • Ask at the right moment. Right after a successful appointment, delivery or project, when the customer is happiest.
  • Make it easy. Copy the short review link from your profile and send it on WhatsApp with a one-line personal request.
  • Reply to every review. Thank people by name for positive reviews. For negative ones, respond calmly, acknowledge the issue and offer to resolve it offline. Future customers read your replies.
  • Stay within the rules. Don't offer discounts or gifts in exchange for reviews, don't write your own and don't buy them. Google removes suspicious reviews and can penalise profiles.

Step 5: Keep the profile active

Treat your profile like a shop window. Each week or two, publish a short update: a new product, an offer, an event, a seasonal reminder. Watch the questions-and-answers area and answer quickly, or add common questions yourself with clear answers. Check that Google hasn't changed any of your details based on user suggestions, and correct them if needed.

Step 6: Make your details consistent across the web

Your business name, address and phone number should be identical on your website, Facebook, Instagram, directories and any association listings. Inconsistent details confuse customers and weaken the signals Google uses to trust your information. It is worth searching for your business name and old phone numbers once a year and correcting any outdated listings you find.

Your website supports your profile too. A page for each main service, a contact page with your address and map, and local business schema markup all help Google connect the dots.

Step 7: Measure what matters

Google Business Profile shows how many people viewed your profile, searched for you, requested directions, called or clicked to your website. Track those numbers monthly. Calls and direction requests are the closest thing to real customers, so focus on those rather than views. If you can, ask new customers how they found you and note how many mention Google or Maps.

How long does it take?

Completing and correcting a profile can produce improvements within weeks, particularly if it was previously unclaimed or sparse. Building reviews and prominence is a longer game that compounds over months. Competition matters too: a café in a busy commercial area faces more rivals than a specialist clinic in a quieter district.

A quick checklist

  1. Claim and verify your profile.
  2. Choose the most specific primary category.
  3. Complete every relevant field, including services and holiday hours.
  4. Upload at least ten genuine, recent photos.
  5. Send your review link to recent happy customers this week.
  6. Post an update every one to two weeks.
  7. Align your details everywhere else online.
