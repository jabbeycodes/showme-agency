Ask a Ghanaian shopper how they want to pay online and the answer, more often than not, is Mobile Money. Card use is growing, and bank transfers have their place, but MoMo is what most of your customers already use every day. If your website can't take it smoothly, you are asking people to change their habits at the exact moment they are ready to buy.

This guide walks through your options for accepting Mobile Money, card and bank payments on a website, what to look for in a payment provider, and the small details that decide whether a customer completes their order or gives up halfway.

Why MoMo checkout matters

Many small businesses in Ghana still sell through a familiar routine: the customer sends a DM, the business replies with a MoMo number, the customer sends money, then sends a screenshot, and someone checks the wallet before confirming the order. It works, but it has real costs:

  • Lost sales. Every extra step is a chance for the customer to get distracted or change their mind.
  • Manual checking. Someone has to match screenshots to orders, which is slow and easy to get wrong.
  • Fraud risk. Edited screenshots and "pending" payments are a known problem.
  • No records. Without a proper system, it is hard to know what sold, when and to whom.

A proper checkout lets the customer pay with the wallet they already use, confirms the payment automatically and records the order, without anyone in your team lifting a finger.

Your payment options

Mobile Money

The main wallets are MTN MoMo, Telecel Cash and AT Money. A good checkout supports all three, because you don't control which network your customer uses. The typical flow is simple: the customer chooses Mobile Money, enters their number, receives a prompt on their phone and approves the payment with their PIN.

Debit and credit cards

Cards matter for higher-value purchases, corporate buyers and customers in the diaspora paying for family or property back home. Visa and Mastercard are the essentials.

Bank transfer

Useful for large orders and business-to-business sales. Some providers offer "pay with bank" options that confirm automatically; otherwise you will be reconciling manually.

Choosing a payment provider

You don't connect to each mobile network directly. Instead, you use a payment provider (sometimes called a gateway or aggregator) that handles MoMo, cards and sometimes bank payments in one integration. Providers active in Ghana include Paystack, Hubtel, ExpressPay and Flutterwave, among others. When comparing them, look at:

  • Supported methods. All three MoMo networks, plus cards, at minimum.
  • Fees. Most charge a percentage per successful transaction, sometimes with a cap. Fees change, so check each provider's current pricing page rather than relying on old blog posts.
  • Settlement. How quickly money reaches your bank account or wallet, and whether settlement is automatic.
  • Onboarding requirements. Expect to provide business registration documents, a valid ID such as the Ghana Card, and settlement account details. Start this early; approval can take days.
  • Integration options. Ready-made plugins for your website platform, payment links you can send on WhatsApp, and a reliable dashboard for refunds and reports.
  • Support. When a customer says "I paid but it failed," you need a provider that answers.

There is no single best provider for everyone. The right choice depends on your platform, your customers and how you want to receive funds.

Reducing abandoned checkouts

Getting the payment provider connected is only half the job. The design of your checkout decides how many people actually finish. A few principles make a big difference:

  • Keep it short. Ask only for what you need to fulfil the order. A phone number and delivery location are often enough; a full account sign-up rarely is.
  • Put MoMo first. If most customers pay with Mobile Money, make it the default option, not something hidden under "other methods".
  • Show the total early. Unexpected delivery charges at the last step are a common reason people leave. Show delivery costs or zones up front.
  • Design for phones. Most of your customers are on a mobile device, often on a patchy connection. Large buttons, fast pages and no heavy images on the checkout page.
  • Explain the prompt. A short line such as "Approve the payment prompt on your phone to complete your order" prevents confusion and timeouts.
  • Handle failures gracefully. If a payment times out, let the customer retry in one tap without re-entering everything.
  • Confirm instantly. Send an order confirmation by SMS, email or WhatsApp the moment payment succeeds. It reassures the buyer and cuts "did my payment go through?" messages.

Adding WhatsApp reordering

For many Ghanaian businesses, the best customers are repeat customers: the office that orders lunch every week, the boutique client who buys every new collection, the household that restocks the same groceries each month. Making them go through the full website checkout every time adds friction you don't need.

WhatsApp reordering closes that gap. After a first purchase, the customer can message a simple keyword or tap a saved link to repeat their last order, then receives a MoMo payment link right inside the chat. The order lands in your system the same way a website order would. It combines the convenience your customers already like about ordering on WhatsApp with the automatic payment confirmation and record-keeping of a real store.

Security and trust

Customers are rightly cautious about paying online. Reassure them by using a reputable provider so card and wallet details never touch your own server, keeping your site on HTTPS, showing clear contact details and a returns or refund policy, and displaying the payment methods you accept near the checkout button.

A simple launch checklist

  1. Choose a provider and start the onboarding paperwork.
  2. Decide your delivery zones and charges.
  3. Set up the checkout with MoMo as the default option.
  4. Test real payments on each network and with a card, including a failed payment and a refund.
  5. Turn on automatic confirmations.
  6. Add WhatsApp reordering once the store is running smoothly.
