For years, getting found online meant one thing: ranking on the first page of Google. That still matters. But a growing share of customers now skip the list of links entirely. They open ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or the AI summary at the top of a Google search and ask a direct question: "Which dental clinic in East Legon is good for children?" or "Who builds e-commerce websites with MoMo checkout in Accra?"

The assistant answers with a short list of names, or sometimes just one. If your business is not in that answer, the customer may never see you at all. This guide explains how those recommendations are formed and the practical steps a Ghanaian business can take to be named more often.

Why AI answers matter for local businesses

A traditional search results page gives you ten chances to be clicked. An AI answer often gives you two or three. That makes the competition sharper, but it also creates an opening. Many Ghanaian businesses have thin websites, incomplete Google profiles and few reviews. The assistants have little reliable information to work with, so the businesses that do provide clear, consistent information stand out quickly.

There is a second reason to care: the people asking AI tools for recommendations are usually close to a decision. They are not browsing; they are shortlisting. Being named at that moment is worth far more than a casual visit.

How AI assistants decide what to recommend

No one outside the AI companies knows the exact formula, and it changes often. What we can observe is that assistants try to give answers that are accurate, specific and safe to repeat. To do that, they lean on information they can find in several places and that agrees with itself. In practice, they draw on:

  • Your own website, especially pages that clearly state what you do, where you operate and who you serve.
  • Map and business listings such as your Google Business Profile, with its categories, hours, photos and reviews.
  • Reviews and reputation across Google, Facebook and industry directories.
  • Third-party mentions: local news, association member lists, partner websites and roundup articles.

The common thread is clarity and consistency. If your website says you are in Osu, your Google profile says Cantonments and a directory lists a number that no longer works, an assistant has good reason to leave you out.

Step 1: Write answer-ready content

AI tools are built to answer questions, so give them answers. Look at the questions customers actually ask you on WhatsApp and the phone, then publish clear responses on your website. A good answer-ready page:

  • States the answer in the first sentence or two, then adds detail.
  • Names your location and service area in plain words ("We deliver across Accra and Tema").
  • Includes concrete details: prices or price ranges, timelines, what is included, payment methods.
  • Uses headings that mirror real questions ("How much does a school website cost?").

A detailed FAQ page and one strong page per service usually do more than dozens of vague blog posts. Specific beats clever.

Step 2: Add structured data (schema)

Structured data is a small block of code that labels the facts on your page in a format machines read easily: your business name, address, opening hours, services, prices and FAQs. Common types for local businesses are LocalBusiness (or a more specific type such as Dentist or Restaurant), Service and FAQPage.

Even when schema doesn't produce a visible change in search results, it removes guesswork for any system trying to understand your business. Your web developer can add it in an afternoon, and it should be checked with Google's free Rich Results Test.

Step 3: Make your profiles complete and consistent

Pick one exact version of your business name, address and phone number, and use it everywhere. Then work through the places customers and AI tools look:

  • Google Business Profile: correct primary category, every relevant secondary category, full description, opening hours, services with prices where possible, and recent photos.
  • Social profiles: the same name, phone, website link and a one-line description of what you do and where.
  • Directories and associations relevant to your sector, such as professional bodies, chambers of commerce or industry listings.

Including your Ghana Post GPS digital address on your website and profile is a simple extra signal that helps pin down exactly where you are.

Step 4: Build genuine reviews and mentions

Reviews are one of the strongest signals that a business is real and well regarded. Make it easy for happy customers to leave one: send a direct review link on WhatsApp after a completed job, and ask at the moment they are most pleased. Reply to every review, positive or negative, politely and specifically.

Never buy reviews or post fake ones. Apart from breaking platform rules, fake reviews tend to be generic and repetitive, which is exactly the kind of pattern automated systems are designed to discount.

Beyond reviews, look for honest opportunities to be mentioned elsewhere: a feature in a local publication, a listing on a partner's website, a talk at an industry event that gets written up. Each independent mention is another source confirming that you exist and what you do.

Step 5: Track your AI visibility over time

You can't improve what you don't measure. Start a simple monthly check:

  1. Write down 10 to 20 questions a real customer might ask, in their words, including your area ("best caterer for weddings in Kumasi").
  2. Ask each question in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity, and note whether you are mentioned, how you are described and which competitors appear.
  3. Record the results in a spreadsheet each month and look for movement after each change you make.

Expect answers to vary from day to day; that is normal. The trend over several months is what counts. Be wary of anyone who promises guaranteed placement in AI answers. Nobody controls those systems. What you can control is the quality and consistency of the information about your business.

A realistic timeline

Fixing your website content, schema and profiles can be done in a few weeks. Reviews and third-party mentions build more slowly, over months. Because AI tools refresh their information at different speeds, you may see changes in one assistant before another. Treat it like reputation-building: steady, honest work that compounds.

Where to start this week

  • Ask three AI assistants about your service and area, and note what they say today.
  • Correct any inconsistent name, address or phone details you find.
  • Turn your ten most common customer questions into a clear FAQ page.
  • Send your review link to five recent happy customers.
